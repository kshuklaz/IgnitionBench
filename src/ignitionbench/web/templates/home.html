Learning resources

Published, community-vetted sources — the same kind IgnitionBench's own propellant data comes from. Always cross-check numbers here before trusting them with hardware.

Websites

  • Richard Nakka's Experimental Rocketry — the definitive amateur reference; source of the KNSB/KNDX burn-rate data used in this app.
  • ThrustCurve.org — measured thrust curves for every certified commercial motor.
  • National Association of Rocketry (NAR) — safety codes, certification levels, and local clubs.
  • Tripoli Rocketry Association — high-power certification and research launches (where experimental motors may legally fly).

Open-source tools

  • openMotor — solid motor simulator; source of this app's propellant dataset. Great for cross-checking designs.
  • OpenRocket — full rocket design and flight simulation; use it to verify stability and fin sizing.
  • BurnSim — the hobby-standard motor ballistics tool (free, closed-source) — validate against it before building anything.

Books

  • Rocket Propulsion Elements — Sutton & Biblarz. The standard propulsion textbook; where the nozzle math in this app comes from.
  • Handbook of Model Rocketry — G. Harry Stine. The classic start-to-finish hobby guide, NAR-endorsed.
  • Topics in Advanced Model Rocketry — MIT Press. Bridges hobby practice and real engineering analysis.
